## Station soak (more than 36 hours on four stations, not 48)

"On air in one stretch" is the live `seconds_on_air` of each channel (it starts over after any dip). Worker restarts are the logger's "playout worker restarted" count per channel (public / education / government). Slate minutes are minutes in which a channel showed the slate. "Minutes logged" counts only minutes that ran this build, `1.0.0-rc.8`.

What the numbers do and do not say:

- **Four stations were on air for 36 hours or more in one stretch.** The Beelink was restarted by hand from its desktop at 3:43 PM on November 22 (not a fault of the build). Its 21.6 hours are the time since that restart. It had been up about 16 hours before it.
- **Worker restarts and slate minutes cluster at the start.** On the 5060 Ti, R9 and Beelink, the restarts and almost all slate minutes fall in the first hours after the build was installed (the evening of November 21 into the early morning of November 22), plus the Beelink's two restarts at its manual restart. After about 1 AM on November 22 (5060 Ti clock) the 5070 Ti, 5060 Ti and R9 logged no restart and no slate minute. The cause was read from the station logs: on the R9 (restarts at 23:48 to 23:52 on November 21, after the station restarted at 22:51) and on the 5060 Ti (restarts at 23:39 to 23:53 on its clock), every restart line says "No valid source plan is available; generated fallback slate". That is the station being set up: the channels were started before their schedule was loaded. The Beelink's first restart is its install, and its other two are the manual restart. These restarts are from set-up and the manual restart, not from running the build.
- **The 5060 Ti is set to Pacific time**, one hour behind the other stations (Mountain). Times in its log are Pacific; its clock is correct.
- **Each station's soak window starts at its install of this exact build (`d125c16d`), read from the station's install receipt:** 5070 Ti and Beelink (upgrade) finished about 10:48 PM on November 21, the 5060 Ti (clean) at 11:04 PM Mountain, the R9 (clean) at 11:05 PM, and the MSI (clean) at 11:43 PM. The MSI had also run two earlier test builds that carry the same version number (`eeb5ae3c`, `8b37e9fb`) and one `d125c16d` install that failed (exit 136) earlier on November 21. Rows from before 11:43 PM are left out of the MSI figures.
- **The MSI (16 GB, live captions off, as its own health report states) shows a short slate card at some program changes** (70 slate minutes between 3 AM on November 22 and the reading, about one or two an hour). The log shows the channel stays ON_AIR and "now playing: slate" lasts about 10 to 60 seconds, then the next program starts; for example on the government channel at 10:58 AM on November 22 the log shows the next program's audio preparation starting in the same second. That is the next program not being ready in time on a 16 GB machine. Its restarts (10 / 13 / 7) are at install and set-up (the burst around 1 AM on November 22, "No valid source plan is available"), as on the other stations. The service process id did not change in its window. The older minute log dropped 203 minutes, scattered single minutes from about 4:30 PM on November 22; the second logger, the watcher, wrote all 2,301 minutes and reported none missing, so these gaps are in the older log, not outages. Its lowest free memory was 1.5 GB and its CPU peaked at 93.2%. It stayed on air, but it is below the minimum and this is not proof of a supported machine.
- **Installed memory was read from each station's memory modules on 2026-11-23.** The R9 has 32 GB installed (two modules). Windows shows 25.8 GB because the integrated AMD graphics reserve part of it. The Beelink has 64 GB installed (Windows shows 47.8 GB, same reason). The 5070 Ti and 5060 Ti have 32 GB each. The MSI and the laptop have 16 GB each. So the two integrated-graphics stations, the R9 (32 GB) and the Beelink (64 GB), both meet the 32 GB minimum.
- **"Not on air" counts in the raw logs are not outage minutes.** The logger counts every log line that reports a channel moving to a state other than ON_AIR (for example at a program change). These counts are large on some stations (hundreds) while the channels stayed on air in one stretch for 36 hours or more, so they are not used here.
- **Watchdog firings are not counted by the logger.** The logger counts worker restarts and slate minutes instead. A figure for watchdog firings is not given.
- **The 5070 Ti watcher marks its GPU "degraded" for every minute.** Each event says "GPU 0 throttle active: 0x400". `nvidia-smi` on that station (2026-11-23) names bit 0x400 **"Reliability"** and shows it as the only active clock-event reason; power cap, hardware slowdown and thermal slowdown were all not active. That is no sign of a fault. Over the run the GPU peaked at 25% load, 47.4 °C and 40.3 W with 2.3 of 16.3 GB of video memory in use. The watcher's rule is too strict: it should flag only real slowdowns (hardware slowdown, thermal slowdown, power brake, sustained power cap) and not Reliability, Idle or Display-clock bits. Until it is changed, the GPU line in a watcher report reads "degraded, 0%" on a healthy GPU and could hide a real throttle.
- **Machine and CPU:** the 5070 Ti's CPU peaked at 86.3% and its lowest free memory was 10.5 GB. The 5060 Ti's CPU peaked at 89.6% (lowest free memory 15.1 GB), the R9's at 89.5% (15.7 GB) and the Beelink's at 76.4% (31.9 GB).

A sixth machine, a 16 GB laptop (Intel i7-9750H, GTX 1660 Ti 6 GB, captions off), was clean-installed with this build on November 23. Its three channels took 40 to 60 minutes after install to reach real video (first-program preparation was slow on its CPU). Then, from 12:03 to 12:33 PM MST, it showed 0 slate minutes on all three channels and was healthy in every minute. It is below the minimum, was watched for 30 minutes only, and is not proof of a supported machine.

Scope limits, stated plainly:

- This is a soak of **about 36 to 39 hours on four stations and about 22 hours on a fifth, not a 48-hour soak**.
- **No Intel integrated-graphics station was tested.**
- The MSI and the laptop are 16 GB machines, **below the new 32 GB minimum**. They are not proof of a supported machine.

## Minimum machine (owner decision)

The supported minimum is **32 GB of RAM**, with a processor whose integrated graphics are enough. No separate graphics card is required. This supersedes any earlier statement that no minimum hardware specification was published. Model-tier memory figures (for example the 16 GB that the 12B summary model needs) are model requirements and are unchanged.

## Open item: OpenSSL

- OpenSSL 3.5.9 has security advisories dated November 17, 2026.
- The fix version, 3.5.10, had no downloadable release when checked on November 22, 2026.
- Review result (2026-11-22, against the exact shipped files): **29 advisory findings**, **28 not reachable** from the shipped programs, plugins and code, **1 not proven**: `gstreamer-openssl 3.5.9` + `CVE-2026-95690`. `gioopenssl.dll` contains the DTLS entry points; no shipped program, plugin, or CivicCast code was found that calls them; shipped PyGObject could construct a DTLS connection; this was NOT proven at run time; the owner accepted this for rc.8 on 2026-11-22; replace with OpenSSL 3.5.10 when it is released.
- The evidence is **static file evidence only** — import tables, string tables and source read from the shipped files. Nothing was run. The fix ships in a later build when OpenSSL 3.5.10 exists.
- The 29 findings are triaged individually in `security/native-audit-triage.json` (reviewed 2026-11-22, review by 2026-12-22).
